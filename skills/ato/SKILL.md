# Account Takeover

Use for account takeover hypothesis discovery and bounded validation. The central question is **which identity claim is accepted, which proof is required, and where the application binds them at the final state-changing step**. An ATO chain may combine attacker-controlled entry, execution or credential exposure, usable authority, an account-state change, and final control; these are puzzle pieces, **not required phases or an exhaustive list**. Identify the proved piece and the missing edge rather than assuming one implies the next. Use only owned or explicitly approved identities. This skill is an idea map and router, not a fixed checklist or an instruction to load every reference.

## Start from the app

1. Read `general-security-testing-policy`, scope, owned-account context, `/account-testing-policy`, and `live-testing-policy` before live action. Check `~/Shared/scopes/{program}/`, then `~/Shared/bounty_recon/{program}/scope/`; use `/pullscope` if needed. If no scope exists, record `no scope` as the policy requires.
2. Observe the current login and account flows directly before reading broad prior hunt state. Notice every entry point, identity claim, proof, pending transaction, final confirmation, session/account result, and notification. Use the ideas below to recognize possibilities while exploring; they do not limit what may be tested.
3. For **each plausible hypothesis** exposed by the application, open the matching focused reference or specialist only when its detail helps the next decision. More than one lane can fit a flow. If a real primitive stalls before unauthorized control, isolate its missing delivery, authority, binding, or final-proof edge and load `references/chain-composition.md`; a failed edge does not erase the observed primitive or prove another route. Revisit the idea map as new behavior appears, and reconcile plausible untested flows before calling coverage complete. There is **no cap on ideas or tests**; scope, ownership, rate, safety, and evidence govern each action.
4. Query prior target memory only after current observations give a specific flow, URL, account relationship, or hypothesis. Do not preload the old context pack, the full source catalog, or every specialist.

## Understand and question authority

Follow the observed flow and use browser traffic, relevant client code, API contracts, or other available evidence to understand how the app decides who is authenticated and what they may do. A complete architecture map is not a prerequisite for investigating a strong signal. For each meaningful piece ask: **why is it present, who supplies it, what can the actor control, and does the server use it as an identity selector, transaction state, proof, or authority?** Treat the answer as unknown until supported by behavior; a cookie, token, client flag, UI step, or documented prerequisite is not automatically enforced.

Compare an understood baseline with meaningful changes to the observed pieces: remove, replace, reorder, reuse, or combine them; use valid owned proof from another account, purpose, session, transaction, or stage where applicable. Examine interactions as well as individual components. Load `/assumption-testing` for claimed prerequisites and the matching specialist for the mechanism. Ask what an attacker can actually obtain, submit, influence, or cause another context to do, then connect those capabilities to account or security-state impact. Preserve the proven primitive and its missing delivery, authority, binding, or final-proof edge; use `references/chain-composition.md` when that helps the next decision.

Follow the session, claimed identity, proof, pending transaction, final account/session, and notifications independently; one may not determine the others. Verify the actual server-side result, not only a response or intermediate success. A failed variation closes only the tested link. Continue materially distinct plausible paths and research the observed implementation when it can change the next decision; missing fixtures or other external prerequisites are open work with a wake condition, not evidence of safety.

## Idea map — signal → binding question → where to expand

- **Signup, passwordless onboarding, verification, alias, or email/phone change:** Can an unverified or pending identifier, normalized alias, old verification step, or new-account action become proof for an existing account? → `references/identity-lifecycle.md`; route token/reset mechanics to `/password-reset`.
- **Password reset, recovery link/code, or magic login:** Does delivery, token purpose, recipient, final account, browser/mobile handoff, token exposure after opening a valid link, or session revocation disagree? → `/password-reset` and its `references/ato-patterns.md`. Do not duplicate reset payload patterns here.
- **Support-assisted or manual account recovery, when offered:** What identity evidence can support accept, and is staff approval bound to the intended account and subsequent reset/factor change? → `references/factors-sessions.md` for factor binding and `/password-reset` for reset mechanics. Observe published flow first; do not impersonate users or contact support as a test without explicit program permission and owned fixtures.
- **SSO, social login, directory provisioning/SCIM, or account link/unlink:** Does a valid IdP or directory proof attach to the wrong local identity, provider, tenant, or transaction? → `references/federation.md` for sign-in/linking and `references/organization-identity.md` for directory-controlled account attributes and grants.
- **MFA, recovery factor, passkey, or remembered device:** Can factor setup, fallback, challenge completion, or removal occur under weaker or wrong-account proof? → `references/factors-sessions.md`.
- **Pending SMS/phone login, session transfer, account switch, logout, desktop/mobile/API route:** Does a pre-verification credential or alternate-client state later acquire another account's authority? → `references/factors-sessions.md`; load the relevant specialist when the main boundary is session or access control.
- **Invite, organization, domain claim, role, or JIT membership:** Which identity, tenant, invite, and role is the final grant bound to—and does accepting an invite also create a login session? → `references/organization-identity.md`; route object/tenant authorization to `/access-control` or `/idor`.
- **A security-state write, credential leak, or browser entry is real but takeover stalls:** What observed bridge would supply the missing victim delivery, usable authority, final identity binding, or login proof? → `references/chain-composition.md` for a non-exhaustive piece map and one bounded discriminator; route the selected mechanism to its own specialist. A failed preflight, guessed cookie, or unconfirmed email closes only that tested edge.
- **Cross-cutting mismatch at any flow:** Browser request vs server decision, pending transaction vs current session, method/parser/header difference, CSRF, or race can affect the above. Route the *observed* mechanism to `/csrf`, `/race`, `/headers`, `/bypass`, or `/single-request-grabber` as appropriate. Do not treat the mechanism as ATO without account/security-factor impact.

References expand recognition signals into account/proof/transaction questions and evidence thresholds; they are **not mandatory all-at-once reads**. Use `references/chain-composition.md` to reason about a missing edge, and `references/flow-handoff.md` to map a complex flow or hand off to a specialist; neither duplicates the specialist's test mechanics.

## Testing and proof

- Map claim (email, subject, phone, account ID, tenant, invite, device), verifier (password, mailbox, IdP assertion, MFA, current session, admin approval), pending transaction, and final server-side binding.
- Establish a valid baseline, then compare bounded mismatches using owned accounts and the least disruptive fixtures. Check the *resulting server-side* account, linked identity, factor, membership/role, and audit/email side effects; record negative results and continue other plausible hypotheses. A negative result or one successful proof does not exhaust independent flows. Stop escalating a proven path at minimum safe evidence.
- Promote only reproducible unauthorized control of another owned account, wrong-account session/link/reset, security-factor change without required proof, or unauthorized role/membership. UI-only confusion, expected creation, harmless aliases, client flags, response wording, or caller-owned changes without cross-account/security-factor impact are leads, not ATO.

## Stop conditions and evidence

Stop before touching non-owned accounts/resources or private data, brute-forcing codes/tokens, repeated MFA guessing, sending security mail to non-owned recipients, lockouts/inbox flooding, or irreversible security changes to a valuable account. Program rules and live-testing policy always prevail.

Use `/data-store-guidance` and the resolved program family/lane: deliberate tests go to `/attempt-recording-policy`, reusable observed behavior to `/map-store`, private candidates to `/hypothesis-ledger`, handoffs to `/bounty-notes`, and proven findings to `/findings`. Use `/bounty-storage` for retained artifacts. Preserve full URLs/methods, auth state, owned aliases and fixture status, loaded lane, redacted baseline/mutation, resulting binding and notifications, cleanup, and stop condition in their owning records. Never record raw passwords, cookies, bearer/reset tokens or links, OAuth codes, SAML assertions, ID/refresh tokens, MFA secrets/recovery codes, mailbox credentials, or private email bodies.
